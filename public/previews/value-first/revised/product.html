Share a recipe webpage

Open Share in your browser and choose Max Memory. When the page provides supported recipe data, Max keeps its ingredients and instructions in Recipes. The saved recipe stays available even if the webpage changes later.

Photograph a cookbook page

In Collections → Recipes, tap Add to take a photo or choose one from your library. Max reads the page and shows the recipe beside it so you can check the ingredients, quantities, and instructions before saving.

Recipe continues on the next page? Add that page too. Keep the cookbook and page number with the saved recipe.

Find it. Shop for it. Cook it.

Ask about the ingredients in your saved recipes, then open the recipe you want. Read its instructions directly, or use Add to List to put its ingredient lines on an existing shopping list.

Ingredient lines already on that list are skipped, so you can see what was added.

What to know when saving a recipe

Web import depends on what the website provides. Paywalls and pages without usable recipe data may remain saved links. For Apple News, Get Recipe helps you find the publisher’s page to share back to Max.

Reading a cookbook photo needs an internet connection and uses some AI Assistant allowance. The page photo is sent to the AI Assistant for reading, then discarded after you save or cancel. Review the result before keeping it. This captures a written recipe; a photo of a finished dish alone does not supply its recipe.

Supported webpage import and reading saved recipes directly use no AI Assistant allowance. Asking Max questions about recipes uses your conversation allowance.

Where your photos live—and what happens if you delete one

Ordinary personal photo memories point to items in Apple’s Photo Library; Max does not make a second photo library. Keep the original available and allow photo access. If the original is deleted or unavailable, the written memory remains but Max cannot display the picture.

Photos deliberately added to shared spaces are smaller copies that other members download. That is a separate action from attaching a photo to a personal memory.

A trip, a wedding, a conference, a kitchen remodel. Group the photos, notes, and memories that belong to it—even when you captured them on different days.

Start with the experience

Ask Max to create an event, or add one in Collections → Events. Keep it open while it’s happening; close it when it’s done and revisit it later.

Add what belongs

Add notes and file photo memories into the event. Long-press a stored photo and choose Add to Event to connect it to the experience.

Reminders & calendars

Give the thought a next step.

Save a task, ask for a reminder, or put an appointment on your calendar through the conversation.

Reminders and calendar appointments

Ask for a reminder with a specific time to get an Apple Reminders alert on your iPhone. A task saved only in Max has no notification. You can also create linked calendar appointments and request an alert before they start.

Keep plans connected

Items Max creates in Calendar and Reminders stay linked to the memory behind them. Update a linked plan or review changes made in the other app.

Move the dentist appointment to Thursday at 2pm.
Updated the linked calendar appointment.

A task to come back to

“Remember that I need to call the dentist.” Keep a task in Max and check it off when done. Saving a task alone does not schedule an alert.

A reminder with a time

“Remind me Friday at 9am.” Max creates an Apple Reminder. Without a time, Max needs you to supply one before it can schedule the notification.

An appointment on the calendar

Create and update appointments through your selected Apple or Google calendar connection. Ask for an alert before the appointment when you need a prompt.

Calendar and Reminders access must be enabled. Alerts follow the phone’s notification settings. Shared reminders and shared calendar alerts can also help someone else know when it’s time: see the caregiver examples.

Privacy & offline

Know what stays on your phone
and what an online answer needs.

Your personal store is local

Personal memories are stored and searched on your iPhone. Ordinary personal photo memories reference your library’s photos and videos. Shared-space photos use smaller uploaded copies; reading a printed recipe sends that page photo to the AI Assistant for processing.

Online answers use context

Your conversation and relevant context, including recalled memories and recognized photo text, pass through our service to an AI provider. Speech recognition and spoken replies run on your phone.

You control what’s saved

Max doesn’t read your inbox or monitor your messages. You can browse, correct, or delete your memories. Shared spaces contain the information you choose to place there.

What works without a connection?

You can browse and edit local memories, lists, tasks, places, and events. Supported on-device answering can use saved information with fewer capabilities than online conversation. New shared-space updates require a connection.

How are sensitive details handled?

Max has a separate way to enter secrets. They are excluded from home-screen search and Siri access, and are revealed on screen when requested rather than spoken aloud by default.

What happens if I lose my phone?

Your personal memory store is on the phone. Dedicated encrypted backup and personal sync are still being developed; their availability and plan placement will be stated before launch. Do not rely on a future feature to recover today’s store.

How do the plans work?

Basic is planned at $5/month, with a bounded online usage allowance and a limited free trial. The planned shared-space structure lets Premium members create spaces and invited Basic members join, each using their own allowance. Premium pricing and final limits are being finalized.

Can I start now?

Max is currently being tested by an invited group. Public trial access is not open yet. The homepage’s plans and access section will carry availability information.

No account to create. No ads or tracking. This website loads no third-party scripts, fonts, or pixels.
